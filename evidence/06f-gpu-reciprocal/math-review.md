# Bounded reciprocal midpoint review

This is an arithmetic design review, not a native correction or a general correctly rounded reciprocal implementation. The frozen diagnostic domain is positive finite FP32 `0.5 <= d <= 8`, with an initial `r0` assumed within one adjacent FP32 value of the exact `1/d`. That assumption must be checked against the exact-rational oracle for every tested input. None of the results below establishes execution on a GPU.

## Why a rounded Newton correction is insufficient here

For the FP32 predecessors of denominators 1, 2, and 4, the exact reciprocal lies only about `3.55e-15`, `1.78e-15`, and `8.88e-16` above the midpoint between the power-of-two reciprocal and its upper neighbor. The existing compensated FMA helper loses this low-order distinction in its final correction on the host. A midpoint comparison can retain that distinction without constructing a rounded Newton result.

Let `r0` be the native initial reciprocal, `p = fl(d*r0)`, and `err = exact(d*r0)-p`. A Dekker TwoProduct split can recover `err` using supported FP32 multiplication, addition, and subtraction, provided those operations retain the specified order and no intermediate underflows. In this bounded domain, the products stay normal. Because `p` is near 1, `h = 1-p` is exact by Sterbenz's lemma. Let `u` and `l` be the upward and downward FP32 gaps adjacent to `r0`. They differ at exact powers of two: `l=u/2` there, and `l=u` elsewhere. Since each half-gap is a power of two, `d*(u/2)` and `d*(l/2)` are exact FP32 exponent scalings in this domain.

The exact comparison with the upper midpoint is the sign of `h - (err + d*u/2)`. The lower comparison is the sign of `h - (err - d*l/2)`. Use error-free TwoSum for each parenthesized sum, retaining both its high and low parts. If `hi` is the correctly rounded FP32 high part of the exact sum, compare `h` and `hi` first: a distinct representable `h` is strictly on one side of that exact sum. Only when `h == hi` does the low part decide. An exact zero is a midpoint tie, to be resolved by even significand parity. If the upper comparison is positive, choose the upper neighbor; if the lower comparison is negative, choose the lower neighbor; otherwise keep `r0`, subject to the tie rule.

Do not derive the reciprocal binade from approximate `log2`: the FP32 value immediately below a power can be rounded to that exponent. For this narrow domain, exact comparisons with powers of two can select the binade and the upward gap. Detect `r0` equal to its binade power to halve the downward gap. The significand parity can be obtained from the exact integer `r0 / upward_gap` in this range, with explicit power-boundary handling. The generated neighbor values and the tie decision need exact-rational tests at each binade edge.

I independently simulated a conventional FP32 Dekker TwoProduct and TwoSum comparator on 30,030 `(d,r0)` pairs: 10,000 fixed-seed denominators in `[0.5,8]`, exact powers and their FP32 predecessors, each with the rounded reciprocal and its two immediate neighbors. For every pair, `p+err` equaled the exact rational product, and the comparator sign matched the exact rational midpoint comparison on both sides. This is host arithmetic evidence only. It does not validate Core ML's optimizer, compiler lowering, flush-to-zero policy, or operation order. The three predecessors of 1, 2, and 4 specifically require the upper neighbor; their exact midpoint margins above are much smaller than an ordinary FP32 product rounding unit.

For a native qualification, freeze the helper and exact input set before compilation. Require the initial reciprocal to reproduce the saved values, retain the intended split-product and TwoSum operations in the converted MIL, and expose `p`, `err`, half-gap products, high/low sums, comparisons, and final selection as outputs. Test exact-rational oracle agreement and unchanged CPU bytes on captured denominators, binade predecessors/successors, exact powers, and midpoint parity cases. After those isolated controls, a separate group-0 clone still needs the same saved-input/state CPU preservation and GPU-policy endpoint/state controls. No full-model fidelity or speed conclusion follows from this review.
