# Independent 06d preflight review

Read-only review of the signed ties-to-even helper, the isolated operator probe, the structural group-0 builder/preflight, and the controlled group replay worker. No native calls or source/artifact edits were made by this review.

The explicit magnitude/parity helper is algebraically consistent with ties-to-even on finite FP32 quotient inputs. Its sign restoration handles negative nonzero values rounding to zero and preserves literal signed zero in Torch. I independently compared `Q_signed_even` with the sealed `graphs.Q` on 408 directed inputs covering both signs around ties and neighboring FP32 values, zeros/underflows, and signed neighbors of powers of two; the outputs were bitwise identical, including 18 negative zeros. It also matched `graphs.Q` bitwise on the captured raw `x_9`, `x_27`, `x_29`, and `x_31` arrays from both chunks/policies. This validates the Torch helper only.

The isolated Core ML operator attempt (`operator-1`) completed with 14/40 cases failing. I re-read every saved output against its reference: all mismatches are exclusively negative-zero sign changes (`0x80000000` expected, `0x00000000` actual); no nonzero values differ. Among the tested query lengths, Q=1 passes and retains all five negative-zero outputs, while directed Q=3/Q=12/Q=32 fail on 15/60/160 negative-zero outputs for both CPU_ONLY and CPU_AND_GPU; Q=2 and Q=4 were not tested. Captured `x_29` loses all 1,965 cold or 761 warm negative-zero signs for both source trajectories and both policies. Therefore the native operator gate is a no-go for strict Torch byte parity on these tested lengths; compute policy does not prove device placement. A later independent original-Q control resolves attribution: all 40 output arrays from operator-1, operator-2, and the unmodified sealed `graphs.Q` are byte-identical for each matching input/policy. Against Torch, the original Q control has the same 11,374 negative-zero-to-positive-zero mismatches; its 10 Q=1 expected negative zeros are preserved. The tested full-Q signed-zero discrepancy predates both replacement candidates; do not attribute it to their helper or infer the exact native stage where signs are lost. Keep all cases as evidence and maintain a separate candidate-versus-original native CPU gate.

The structural builder preflight is sound within its stated structural scope: all 131 original group-0 `round` operations were replaceable, 42,367 other operations remained byte-identical, output names/types and input/output descriptors were preserved, state/weight metadata remained unchanged, and an unknown site was rejected. It performs no numerical qualification. The group replay worker now checks the source candidate/helper/builder hashes, full 131-site replacement map, package/compiled inventories, source/cache identity, fixture/runtime provenance, state restoration, and per-call output/cache arrays. Its original-CPU/candidate-CPU/candidate-CPU_AND_GPU arms use the same saved CPU inputs and eight-state checkpoints; CPU exactness is mandatory, and GPU-policy results are compared to the original CPU reference. Those controls are appropriate after the isolated operator passes. The Torch-traced operator gate and direct MIL helper inserted by the structural builder are distinct implementations; the group CPU replay remains the separate check for the MIL replacement.

The original full-model gates and tolerances remain unchanged. Actual placement remains unverified, and no full-model GPU conclusion follows from these local checks.

## Separate v2 operator preflight

The revised `signed_even_round_v2.py` avoids selecting signed zero by deriving `sign_factor = z / select(abs(z) == 0, 1, abs(z))` and multiplying the nonnegative rounded magnitude by it. On finite quotient inputs, that produces an exact ±1 sign factor for nonzero values and retains the sign of literal zero through division by positive one. I independently checked `Q_signed_even_v2` against sealed `graphs.Q` on 410 directed FP32 inputs, including ties/nextafter neighbors, positive/negative zero, tiny values, and the clamp half-step ±2^-108 with adjacent FP32 values; all outputs matched bitwise, including signed zeros. All captured `x_9`, `x_27`, `x_29`, and `x_31` arrays also matched bitwise under the Torch helper.

The frozen `operator-2` probe now imports the v2 helper, pins the v2 helper/probe/protocol hashes and prior operator result, and verifies that its 20 test inputs have the exact same hashes as operator-1 before making 40 native predictions. The coverage retains Q=1/3/12/32 plus all captured arrays, and exact FP32 bit comparison includes zero signs. No blocker was found in the finite-input or case-binding path. Its `compare()` example serialization still emits Python float values under `allow_nan=False`; if a native result unexpectedly contains NaN/Inf, report updates could fail before the nonfinite gate is recorded. This is a diagnostic robustness improvement, not an observed v2 numerical issue.

The operator-2 candidate is still distinct from the direct MIL helper inserted by `build_signed_even_group_v2.py`. A passing Torch-traced isolated operator would not qualify that MIL replacement; the later group replay must retain its same-input/cache controls, mandatory original-CPU exactness, and strict GPU-policy gate. Neither this preflight nor CPU_AND_GPU policy establishes actual GPU execution.

Keep operator-level Torch parity and full-Q CPU preservation against the original native baseline as distinct claims.
